## 9. Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Expecting pixel-perfect layout on complex PDFs** — PDFs with multi-column layouts, heavy design, or custom fonts will convert with some formatting shifts in Word. The text content will be accurate but spacing and visual layout may need a quick tidy-up. This is true of every PDF-to-Word converter, not just PDFly.

**Converting a scanned PDF directly without OCR first** — Scanned PDFs are images, not text. Converting them to Word without OCR just gives you a Word file with images of pages — not editable text. Use OCR first, then convert.

**Not saving the Word file before editing** — Once you download the .docx and close your browser tab, the file only exists in your downloads folder. Make sure to save it properly before making changes, so you don't lose edits.

For best results on scanned documents, run **PDFly OCR PDF** first to make the text layer selectable, then use the PDF-to-Word tool. Directly converting a scanned PDF gives you image-only output in Word.

**Will the fonts and formatting look the same in Word?**
Standard fonts (Arial, Times New Roman, Calibri) transfer well. Custom or embedded fonts may substitute to the closest available font on your system. Formatting like bold, italic, and paragraph breaks are preserved accurately.

**Does conversion work on PDFs with tables and bullet points?**
