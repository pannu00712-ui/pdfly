## 8. Pro Tip 💡

If your PowerPoint uses custom fonts downloaded from the internet, embed them before converting (File → Options → Save → Embed fonts in the file). This ensures the fonts render correctly in the PDF output rather than substituting to a default system font.

If the converted PDF is large due to high-resolution slide images, use **PDFForge Compress PDF** after conversion — still browser-based, no upload at any point.

## 9. Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Converting a deck that still has speaker notes you didn't mean to include** — Standard PPT-to-PDF conversion includes slides only (not notes) by default, but double-check your output. If notes pages appear, re-convert with notes excluded.

**Not embedding fonts before converting** — Custom fonts that aren't embedded may substitute to Arial or another system font in the PDF. The slide content stays correct but the visual style can shift noticeably.

**Sending a large uncompressed PDF after converting image-heavy slides** — Slides with full-bleed photos or high-res graphics can produce very large PDFs. Run a quick compress pass before emailing if file size is a concern.

## 10. FAQ

**Will slide animations and transitions appear in the PDF?**
No — PDFs are static. Each slide becomes one PDF page with its final design state. Animations and transitions don't carry over, which is expected behavior for any PPT-to-PDF conversion.

**Does the PDF include slide numbers?**
Slide numbers that are set up in your PowerPoint (Insert → Slide Number) will appear in the PDF. PDF page numbers are separate and may not match depending on your viewer settings.

**Can I convert a .ppt file (older format), or only .pptx?**
PDFForge works with .pptx. For older .ppt files, open them in PowerPoint or Google Slides and save as .pptx first, then convert.

**Will embedded videos in my slides convert?**
Videos don't carry into PDF — they're dynamic content. The slide will show the video's thumbnail or poster image in the PDF output.

**Is there a slide count limit?**
No limit from PDFForge. Very large decks (100+ slides with high-res images) may take slightly longer to process in-browser, but will convert fully.
