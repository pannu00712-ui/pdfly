How to Convert Word to PDF: Preserve Your Documents for Sharing

By Alex Rivera · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

You've written a document in Word. It's perfectly formatted, with custom fonts, tables, and images. You send it to someone, and they open it on their computer. The fonts change. The table breaks across pages. The images shift. Your perfectly crafted document looks like a mess.

This is the Word curse. Word is an editor, not a layout tool. When you open a .docx file in a different version of Word — or in Google Docs, or on a different operating system — things move around. A PDF fixes all of that.

Word to PDF is probably the most common conversion people do. It's the foundation of document sharing in business, education, and personal communication. Yet many people still do it the hard way.

Why Word-to-PDF conversion matters

Word is great for creating content. PDF is great for presenting it. Here's why you convert:

  • Lock formatting: What you see is what they get. No font changes, no layout shifts. The document looks the same everywhere.
  • Professional appearance: PDFs look more polished than Word files. They're what clients expect.
  • Protect from editing: A PDF is harder to edit, which is useful for final documents. This protects against accidental changes.
  • Universal viewing: PDFs open correctly on any device, any operating system. This is the key advantage.

There's also a legal dimension. In many legal contexts, PDF is the preferred format because it's harder to alter without leaving traces. This is why contracts are almost always sent as PDFs.

No software needed: Convert Word to PDF

You don't need to open Word and use "Save As PDF." The whole conversion happens right in your browser.

  1. Open a tool like PDFly's Word to PDF.
  2. Upload your .docx or .doc file.
  3. Wait a few seconds while the tool converts the document.
  4. Download your PDF — ready to share, print, or archive.

One advantage of using a dedicated tool over Word's built-in export is that dedicated tools often handle complex formatting better. Word's export can sometimes struggle with tables, footnotes, or custom styles. Specialized tools are designed to handle these cases.

What's preserved in the conversion?

  • Text: Fonts, sizes, colors, and styling. All text formatting is preserved.
  • Images: Photos, diagrams, and graphics. These are embedded in the PDF.
  • Tables: Table structure and content. Tables remain tables in the PDF.
  • Layout: Margins, page breaks, and element positioning. The PDF should look like the Word document.
  • Headers and footers: Page numbers, titles, and other repeating elements. These are preserved.

What might not transfer perfectly:

  • Track changes: Track changes are typically not visible in the PDF. The final version of the text is shown.
  • Complex Word-specific features: Some advanced Word features may not have exact PDF equivalents. This includes things like certain types of embedded objects.

The conversion is usually lossless. If the Word document is clean, the PDF will be identical. If the Word document has complex or unusual features, the PDF might differ slightly.

Word to PDF vs. PDF to Word

These are opposites, and each has its purpose:

  • Word to PDF: Take an editable document and lock it into a fixed layout. Use when you're ready to share the final version.
  • PDF to Word: Take a fixed document and make it editable again. Use when you need to make changes to a PDF.

The Word-to-PDF direction is much more reliable. It's a one-way street that preserves everything. The PDF-to-Word direction is more error-prone because the conversion has to reconstruct the document structure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my fonts look correct in the PDF?

Yes — fonts are embedded in the PDF, so they'll display correctly even if the viewer doesn't have the font installed.

Can I convert a scanned Word document to PDF?

If you have a scanned image, you'd need to OCR it first. But a proper .docx file converts directly without any extra steps.

Can I convert a PDF back to Word?

Yes — you can use a PDF-to-Word tool to convert the PDF back, though some formatting may need adjustment.
