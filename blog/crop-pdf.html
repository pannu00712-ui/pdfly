How to Crop a PDF: Trim Margins, White Space, and Unwanted Areas

By Alex Rivera · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

You've got a PDF with huge margins — someone must have printed it on A4 and then scanned it back. The actual content is in the center, surrounded by a sea of white space. Or maybe you've got a screenshot embedded in a PDF, and you want to trim it down to just the image.

Cropping a PDF isn't like cropping an image. You're not just hiding the edges — you're telling the PDF viewer what to display. The right crop can make your document look cleaner, more professional, and easier to read.

What makes PDF cropping different from image cropping is that the cropped content often remains in the file. It's just hidden from view, rather than deleted. This is both an advantage and a limitation — it means you can reverse the crop, but it also means the file size doesn't necessarily decrease.

Why crop a PDF?

There are a few common reasons to crop a PDF:

  • Remove white space. Large margins waste space and make the document look sloppy.
  • Focus on content. If you only need part of a page, cropping removes the rest.
  • Standardize page sizes. If you have a mix of page sizes, cropping can make them uniform.
  • Remove headers or footers. Cropping can trim off unwanted elements at the top or bottom.

Another use case that's less obvious: preparing documents for mobile viewing. A PDF with generous margins might be perfectly readable on a desktop monitor but cramped on a phone screen. Cropping the margins makes the content larger and more readable on small screens.

Understanding crop boxes

PDFs have something called "crop boxes" — a set of coordinates that defines what's visible. When you crop a PDF, you're changing those coordinates. The cropped-out content still exists in the file, but the viewer doesn't display it.

This is important because it means cropping is reversible — you can uncrop a PDF if you realize you cut off too much. Some tools let you remove the crop and restore the full page.

There are actually multiple box types in a PDF: the media box (the full page), the crop box (what's visible), and sometimes bleed boxes and trim boxes for print production. Most users only need to worry about the crop box.

Cropping vs. trimming

People often use these terms interchangeably, but they're slightly different:

  • Cropping hides the edges of the page. The content is still there, just not visible.
  • Trimming permanently removes the cropped content. It's a more aggressive action.

Most online tools crop rather than trim, which is safer — you can always adjust the crop later.

Trimming is more common in print production, where file size matters and you need to permanently remove everything outside the crop area. For most everyday uses, cropping is the better choice.

If file size reduction is your goal, cropping won't help much. The hidden content is still in the file. You'd need a compression tool or a trim tool that actually removes the hidden content.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I crop just one page of a PDF?

Yes — most crop tools let you select specific pages or page ranges to crop, leaving the rest unchanged.

Does cropping reduce the PDF file size?

It can, especially if you're removing large amounts of white space or images. But the main purpose is visual, not compression.

Can I uncrop a PDF after cropping?

If the crop tool uses crop boxes (rather than trimming), yes — the content is still in the file and can be restored with another tool.
