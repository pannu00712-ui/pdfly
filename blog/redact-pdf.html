How to Redact a PDF: Permanently Remove Sensitive Information

By Alex Rivera · Updated July 2026 · 4 min read

You've got a document that needs to be shared — but it contains sensitive information. A client's name, a bank account number, an address. You could cover it with a black box, but that's not enough. The text is still there, just hidden. Anyone with basic PDF skills could remove the box and see the information.

Redaction is different. Redaction permanently removes the text from the PDF. It's not hidden — it's gone. There's no way to recover it. This is the only way to truly protect sensitive information.

Redaction vs. blacking out

This is the most important distinction to understand:

  • Blacking out (overlay): A black box is placed over the text. The text is still in the file, just covered. It can be removed.
  • Redaction: The text is permanently deleted from the file. It cannot be recovered. The space is left blank.

When to redact a PDF

  • Legal documents: Remove client names, case numbers, or confidential details.
  • Financial statements: Remove bank account numbers, social security numbers, or tax IDs.
  • Medical records: Remove patient names, dates of birth, or other PHI.
  • Internal documents: Remove proprietary information before sharing externally.

What gets redacted?

  • Selected text: Any text you highlight or select for redaction.

  • Images: Redaction typically applies to text, not images. If you have sensitive information in images, you'd need to remove or cover the image.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can redacted text be recovered?

No — redaction permanently removes the text. It's not hidden or covered; it's deleted from the file.

Can I redact images in a PDF?

Most redaction tools focus on text. For images, you'd need to remove or cover the image separately.

Can I redact all instances of a word at once?

Yes — many redaction tools let you search for a term and redact every instance throughout the document.
