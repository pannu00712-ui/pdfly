How to Rotate a PDF: Fix Sideways Pages Instantly

By Alex Rivera · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

You've scanned a document, and page 3 is sideways. Page 7 is upside down. The rest are fine. You could rescan the whole thing, but that means starting over. Or you could just rotate those pages and be done with it.

Rotating PDF pages is one of the simplest fixes, but many tools make you rotate the entire document — not just specific pages. With the right tool, you can rotate one page, a range of pages, or the whole document.

It's a small fix, but it matters — seems trivial but can be surprisingly annoying when you use the wrong tool. Some PDF readers let you rotate the view temporarily, but the rotation doesn't save. You need a tool that actually changes the page orientation in the file.

Why pages get rotated

It happens more often than you'd think:

  • Scanning: Documents go through the scanner face-down or in the wrong orientation. This is the most common cause.
  • Exporting: Some apps export PDFs in portrait when you wanted landscape, or vice versa. This happens with presentations and spreadsheets.
  • Mixed orientation: A document might have both portrait and landscape pages. This is common with reports that include wide tables or charts.
  • Viewing differences: A PDF might look correct in one viewer but sideways in another. This usually indicates an orientation flag that isn't being read correctly.

There's also a less common but frustrating cause: PDFs created from photos. If you take a photo of a document and convert it to PDF, the orientation depends on how you held the phone.

Get it done in seconds: Rotate a PDF

You don't need to open the PDF in an editor or use print settings. A dedicated tool lets you rotate pages in seconds.

  1. Open a tool like PDFly's Rotate PDF.
  2. Upload your PDF.
  3. Choose your rotation — 90°, 180°, or 270°.
  4. Select which pages to rotate — all pages, specific pages, or a range.
  5. Click process and download your rotated PDF.

Simple as that. The pages are rotated exactly as you specified.

One advantage of using a dedicated tool is that you can rotate pages without rendering the document first. This is faster and works with PDFs that might be slow to open in a viewer.

Which rotation to use

  • 90° clockwise: Rotates the page right. This is what you need when a page is rotated 90° to the left.
  • 90° counter-clockwise: Rotates the page left. This is what you need when a page is rotated 90° to the right.
  • 180°: Flips the page upside down. This is what you need when a page is completely inverted.
  • 270°: The same as 90° counter-clockwise. Some tools use this terminology instead.

If you're not sure which rotation to use, try 90° first. If that doesn't fix it, try 180°. If that makes it worse, try 270°. Eventually you'll find the right one.

Rotating specific pages vs. the whole document

This is the key feature you need. Many tools only let you rotate all pages at once. But if you have a document with mixed orientations — portrait pages 1-5, landscape pages 6-7, portrait 8-10 — you need to rotate just the landscape pages.

A good tool lets you select:

  • All pages — rotate the entire document.
  • Even pages — rotate every even-numbered page. This is useful for facing pages.
  • Odd pages — rotate every odd-numbered page. This is the opposite of even pages.
  • Custom pages — select specific page numbers or ranges. This gives you full control.

The even/odd feature is particularly useful for double-sided documents where you want all pages to be in the same orientation after printing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I rotate just one page in a PDF?

Yes — most tools let you select specific pages to rotate, leaving the rest unchanged.

Does rotating a PDF affect the file quality?

No — rotation is a simple orientation change. The content, quality, and formatting remain exactly the same.

Can I rotate a scanned PDF?

Yes — scanned PDFs are just images, and rotation works the same way as with any other PDF.
