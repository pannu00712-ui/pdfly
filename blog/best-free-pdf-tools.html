7 Free PDF Tools I Actually Use (And Why I Dropped Adobe)

I paid for Adobe Acrobat for almost three years. Every renewal notice made me wince a little more, mostly because I could never remember what I actually used it for beyond rotating a page here, deleting a page there, and occasionally merging two contracts before sending them off. Eventually I cancelled it out of spite and spent a weekend testing whatever free alternatives I could find. Some were awful. A few were genuinely better than what I'd been paying for.

This isn't a "top 10 tools ranked by an algorithm" post. It's what actually stuck around on my computer after the testing was done, and what I'd tell a friend who asked me the same question I used to ask myself: do I really need to pay for this?

The short answer: probably not

Most people only ever do four things with a PDF — read it, sign it, merge a couple of files, or shrink one down so it'll actually attach to an email. None of that requires a subscription anymore. It used to, back when Adobe had the market mostly to itself, but that hasn't been true for a while.

Where it gets more complicated is if you're editing text inside a PDF, running OCR on a scanned document, or working with anything sensitive like a signed contract or a medical form. That's where a lot of "free" tools quietly show their limits — either a watermark shows up, or you find out the file was uploaded to a server somewhere you didn't think about.

For actual editing: PDFgear surprised me

I went in expecting PDFgear to be another bloated free-with-upsells app, and it mostly isn't. You open a PDF, click into the text, and it lets you edit it directly — it even does a decent job matching the original font, so your edit doesn't look like it was pasted in with a different typewriter. No watermark on export, which after using Adobe was honestly the part that surprised me most.

It also has OCR built in, so a scanned document can become searchable and editable text, and there's an AI assistant tucked into the sidebar for summarizing or translating a document if you need that. I don't use that feature much, but it's there.

The one thing I'd flag: PDFgear has had some chatter online about how it handles data privacy, so I wouldn't run anything genuinely sensitive through it without reading their current policy first. For a resume or a form you're filling out, it's fine.

If you're on Windows and don't need to edit text: PDF24

PDF24 Creator has been around forever, in internet years, and it still does exactly what it did five years ago: merge, split, compress, convert, and OCR, all offline, all free, no watermark. It can't touch the actual text inside a PDF — for that you need something like PDFgear — but if your day-to-day is mostly reorganizing pages or shrinking a file so it clears an email size limit, this is the tool that just works without asking anything of you.

It also installs itself as a virtual printer, which is a small thing but genuinely useful — you can "print" almost anything on your computer straight to a PDF without needing a separate converter.

For quick one-off edits without installing anything: Sejda

Sejda is a browser tool, and it's one of the only ones I've found that edits existing text in a PDF without stamping a watermark across it. It reads the font in the document and tries to match it, which mostly works. The catch is the free version caps you at three tasks an hour and a 50MB file size — fine for a single document, annoying if you're batch-processing anything.

I use this specifically for the "I need to fix one typo on a form right now and I'm not at my main computer" situation. It's not my daily driver.

The one I keep coming back to for privacy: BentoPDF

BentoPDF is worth mentioning because of how it's built, not just what it does. It's a web tool, but the processing happens in your browser rather than on a server somewhere — your file never actually gets uploaded anywhere. For anything I'd hesitate to drop into a random online converter, this is the one I trust. It's also got a surprisingly deep toolset for something this lightweight, and if you're technical enough to self-host it, that's an option too.

What I'd actually tell you to install

If you want one tool and don't want to think about it further: get PDFgear. It covers editing, merging, splitting, OCR, and conversion, and it's free without the usual catch. If you're on Windows and mostly reorganizing files rather than editing text inside them, PDF24 is faster for that specific job and completely offline.

Keep Sejda bookmarked for the rare moment you need to edit a PDF on a computer that isn't yours. And if privacy is the deciding factor for you — say you're dealing with contracts, medical paperwork, or anything you wouldn't want sitting on someone else's server — BentoPDF or an offline tool like PDF24 are the safer calls.

A few things I'd tell you to watch out for

Watermarks are the most common trap. A lot of "free" editors will let you make every change you want and then quietly stamp their logo across the exported file — always do a test export before you commit to a tool for something that matters.

Usage limits are the second trap. Tools like Sejda cap you at a handful of actions per hour, which is fine for occasional use but will frustrate you fast if you're working through a stack of documents.

And the biggest one: don't upload anything sensitive to a browser-based tool unless you've actually checked whether it processes files locally or on a server. Most people never check, and most of the time it doesn't matter — until it does.
