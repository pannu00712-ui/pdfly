How to Convert PNG to PDF: The Easy Way to Combine Images

By Alex Rivera · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

You've got a set of PNG images — maybe they're screenshots, scanned documents, or design mockups. And you need to send them as a single PDF. You could paste them into Word, but that's clunky. Or you could convert them directly to a PDF.

PNG to PDF is one of the simplest conversions, but it's incredibly useful. It turns a collection of images into a single, professional document that's easy to share, print, and archive.

PNG is the preferred format for many design and web workflows because it's lossless and supports transparency. But when you need to present or share multiple PNGs, a PDF is usually the better choice. It's more professional, easier to navigate, and more widely supported.

Why PNG to PDF?

PNG is great for images — it's lossless, sharp, and supports transparency. But when you need to combine multiple images into a document, PDF is better.

  • One file: Instead of 10 separate images, you have one PDF. This is easier to manage, share, and store.
  • Professional: PDFs look more polished than a folder of images. They're what clients expect for formal documents.
  • Print-friendly: PDFs are designed for printing. Images alone can be difficult to print correctly.
  • Shareable: PDFs work everywhere, unlike some image formats. You can open a PDF on any device.

There's also a practical benefit: PDFs support features that images don't. You can add bookmarks, create a table of contents, or add metadata. These features are useful for longer documents.

One thing to note: PNGs are often larger than JPGs. If you're converting many PNGs, the resulting PDF might be large. If file size is a concern, consider using JPGs instead, or use the compression setting if the tool offers it.

What about image quality?

PNG is lossless, and a good converter preserves that quality. Your images will look exactly the same in the PDF as they did in the original PNG files.

This is the key advantage of PNG over JPG. There's no recompression. The PNG data is embedded directly into the PDF, so the quality is preserved exactly.

PNG to PDF vs. JPG to PDF

Both do the same thing, but with different image formats:

  • PNG: Lossless, larger files, supports transparency. Best for text, diagrams, and graphics.
  • JPG: Compressed, smaller files, no transparency. Best for photos and large images.

Use PNG to PDF when image quality is critical or when your images have text or transparency. Use JPG to PDF when file size matters more.

There's also a consideration around color depth. PNG supports 8-bit and 24-bit color, and sometimes 48-bit. JPG supports 24-bit. For most uses, the difference doesn't matter. For professional design work, PNG's higher color depth might be relevant.

When PNG is the right choice

PNG is the right choice when you need to preserve exact quality and your images contain text, diagrams, or sharp lines. This includes screenshots, scanned documents, design mockups, and logos. PNG's transparency support is also valuable for overlays and composite images.

If your images are photographs or contain lots of color gradients, JPG might be a better choice. The compression is designed for these types of images and won't be as noticeable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I convert multiple PNGs into a single PDF?

Yes — you can upload as many PNGs as you need, and they'll all be combined into one PDF with each image on its own page.

Will my PNGs keep their transparency in the PDF?

PNG's transparency is preserved in the PDF. The PDF will display transparent areas as transparent, not white or black.
