How to Compare Two PDFs: Spot Differences Instantly

By Alex Rivera · Updated July 2026 · 4 min read

You've just received a revised contract from a client. It's supposed to be the same as the version you sent, just with one clause updated. But you're not sure — did they change anything else? You could read through both documents side by side, page by page, line by line. Or you could let a tool do the comparison for you.

Comparing PDFs is one of those tasks that sounds simple but is incredibly tedious to do manually. With the right tool, you can spot every difference in seconds — text changes, image shifts, formatting tweaks, and more.

Why compare PDFs?

There are many situations where you need to compare two versions of a document:

  • Contracts: Check that the other side didn't change terms without telling you.
  • Reports: Compare draft and final versions to verify edits.
  • Regulatory documents: Ensure updates haven't removed required content.
  • Design reviews: Check that changes were applied correctly to all pages.

The fastest way to compare PDFs

You don't need to open two windows and scroll back and forth. A dedicated tool does the comparison for you.

  1. Open a tool like PDFly's Compare PDF.
  2. Upload the first PDF (the original version).
  3. Upload the second PDF (the revised version).
  4. Click process — the tool highlights all differences.
  5. Download a report or view the differences side by side.

That's it. The tool does the heavy lifting and shows you exactly what changed.

What types of differences are detected?

A good comparison tool can detect several types of changes:

  • Text changes: Added, removed, or modified text.
  • Image differences: Images that were added, removed, or changed.
  • Formatting changes: Font changes, color shifts, or layout adjustments.
  • Page structure: Pages that were added, removed, or reordered.

How the comparison works

The tool analyzes both PDFs at the content level. It looks at the text, images, and layout of each page, then compares them. Any differences are highlighted — often with color coding (red for removed content, green for added content).

Comparison vs. version tracking

  • Compare PDFs: Shows you the differences between two files. One-off analysis.
  • Version tracking: Keeps a history of changes over time. More complex.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I compare PDFs with different page counts?

Yes — the tool will compare what it can. If one PDF has extra pages, those will be flagged as additions.

Does the comparison tool detect formatting changes?

Yes — it detects font changes, color differences, and layout shifts, not just text changes.

Can I compare scanned PDFs?

If the PDFs are scanned images, the tool may not be able to compare text. You'd need to OCR them first.
