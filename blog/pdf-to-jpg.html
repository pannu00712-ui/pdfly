How to Convert PDF to JPG (Without Losing Quality)

By Alex Rivera · Updated July 2026 · 4 min read

You need to include a page from a PDF in a presentation — but PowerPoint doesn't handle PDFs well. Or you want to share a single page on social media. Or you need to convert a PDF to images for a website.

Converting PDF to JPG turns each page into a high-quality image. It's simple, fast, and doesn't require any special software.

Why PDF to JPG?

There are several reasons you might need to convert a PDF to JPG:

  • Presentations: Images are easier to embed in PowerPoint or Google Slides.
  • Social media: Most platforms handle images better than PDFs.
  • Websites: You can't embed a PDF directly in most web pages.
  • Printing: Some printers handle JPGs more reliably than PDFs.

What about quality?

Quality depends on the settings you choose. Higher quality means larger file sizes but sharper images. For most uses, "high" quality gives a good balance — clear text and images without creating massive files.

The tool converts at the original resolution, so the JPGs should look just as good as the PDF did.

PDF to JPG vs. PDF to PNG

Both are useful, but they're for different things:

  • JPG: Smaller file size, good for photos and complex images. Can lose some quality with compression.
  • PNG: Larger files, but lossless — text stays crisp and sharp. Better for documents with text or diagrams.

If you need perfect text clarity, use PNG. If you need smaller files, use JPG.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I convert specific pages to JPG, not all pages?

Most tools let you select a page range — so you can convert just pages 1-5 or only specific pages.

Will text remain sharp in the JPG?

Text stays clear at high quality settings. If it looks blurry, try increasing the quality or resolution setting.

Can I convert a JPG back to PDF?

Yes — you can use a JPG-to-PDF tool to combine images back into a PDF file. It's a two-way street.
