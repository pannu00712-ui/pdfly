Why run OCR on a PDF?

A scanned PDF looks like a normal document, but each page is really just a picture — you can't search it, copy from it, or highlight a word. OCR reads the printed text in those page images and turns it into text you can select, copy, and search, which is especially useful for digitizing paper records or pulling information out of an archived file.

This tool uses AI to read the page images, so each page is sent to PDFly's server for processing rather than staying entirely on your device — unlike most of PDFly's other tools, which run fully in your browser.

Best Practices & Limitations

Tips for best results

  • Run this on scanned documents before searching or copying text from them — OCR is what makes a scan searchable.
  • Higher-resolution scans (300 DPI+) produce noticeably more accurate OCR than low-resolution ones.
  • Check a few pages after OCR for accuracy, especially on documents with tables, handwriting, or unusual fonts.

Limitations & when not to use this tool

  • Recognition accuracy depends heavily on scan quality — skewed, blurry, or low-contrast scans produce more errors.
  • Handwritten text is recognized far less reliably than printed text.
  • Very large multi-hundred-page scans can take a while since OCR runs page by page.

Frequently asked questions

What kinds of PDFs does this work on?

Scanned documents, photographed pages, and image-based PDFs where the text can't normally be selected or copied.

How many pages can I process?

Up to 6 pages per PDF on the free tool. Longer documents can be split first, or processed a few pages at a time.

Is my PDF uploaded anywhere?

Yes. Because this tool uses AI to read the page images, your pages are sent to PDFly's server for processing rather than staying entirely on your device.

Will my original PDF be changed?

No. Your original file stays unchanged. The extracted text is returned separately, and you decide what to do with it.
