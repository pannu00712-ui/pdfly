Summary

AI-generated summaries may omit nuance. Always check the original document for critical details.

Why summarize a PDF?

Long documents often bury a handful of key points in pages of supporting detail. Instead of reading the whole thing to find what matters, a summary gives you the main ideas up front — useful when you're researching a topic, comparing several reports, or getting ready for a meeting.

This tool uses AI to generate the summary, so the text extracted from your PDF is sent to PDFly's server for processing — unlike most of PDFly's other tools, which run fully in your browser.

Best Practices & Limitations

Tips for best results

  • Use it as a first pass to decide which sections deserve a full read, not as a replacement for reading critical documents.
  • Ask for a shorter or longer summary if the default length doesn't fit your needs.
  • Works best on well-structured documents (reports, articles) rather than dense legal or highly technical text.

Limitations & when not to use this tool

  • AI-generated summaries can occasionally omit or misstate details — verify anything important against the source.
  • This tool sends document content to a server for AI processing, unlike PDFly's browser-only tools.
  • Not a substitute for a full read on contracts, medical documents, or anything with legal or safety implications.

Frequently asked questions

How long can my PDF be?

Text up to roughly 40,000 characters, about 15-20 pages of dense text, is summarized in one pass; longer documents are truncated to that limit.

Does it work on scanned PDFs?

Only if the PDF already has a text layer. Purely scanned or image-only PDFs need OCR first — try PDFly's OCR PDF tool beforehand.

Is my document's text sent anywhere?

Yes. This tool uses AI to generate the summary, so the text extracted from your PDF is sent to PDFly's server for processing.

Does summarizing change my PDF?

No. Your original document stays unchanged. The summary is generated and returned separately.
