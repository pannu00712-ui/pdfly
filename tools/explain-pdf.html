How PDFly's Explain PDF works

Long reports, research papers, and contracts are often dense — full of jargon, footnotes, and structure that takes time to untangle. PDFly's Explain PDF tool speeds that up by turning the document into a short, plain-language summary you can skim in seconds.

The process happens in two stages. First, your browser reads the PDF locally using the pdf.js library and pulls out its text content — the file itself never leaves your device at this step. Second, that extracted text (not the file) is sent to generate an AI explanation, which is returned and displayed on the page. Nothing is stored afterward.

Because the tool works from extracted text, it depends on the PDF having a real text layer. Documents that are photographed or scanned pages saved as images have no text to extract, so there's nothing for the AI to read.

What it's good for

  • Quick orientation: getting the gist of a long report or paper before deciding whether to read it in full.
  • Studying: turning a dense textbook chapter or lecture PDF into plain-language notes.
  • Unfamiliar jargon: breaking down technical, legal, or medical documents written for specialists.

Limitations to know before you rely on it

  • Length cap: only the first ~40,000 characters of text are read — roughly 15–20 typical pages. Anything past that point is not included in the explanation.
  • Not a substitute for the original: the explanation is a summary, not a verified transcript. For contracts, medical documents, or anything with legal or financial consequences, treat it as a starting point and confirm details against the source PDF.
  • Scanned documents: if the PDF is made of page images rather than real text, there's nothing to extract — see Troubleshooting below.

Common mistakes

  • Uploading a scanned PDF and expecting an explanation — run OCR first so there's text to read.
  • Treating the explanation as a complete substitute for the document, especially for anything with fine print.
  • Uploading a very long document and assuming the whole thing was covered — check whether your file exceeds the length cap.

Troubleshooting

  • "Could not extract text" error: the PDF is likely scanned or image-based. Run it through OCR PDF first, then explain the OCR'd result.
  • Explanation seems to stop mid-document: your file likely exceeds the ~40,000 character limit — the label will show "(truncated)" when this happens.
  • "AI capacity reached" or a busy message: the AI service has a shared daily limit. Wait a while and try again, or try again the next day.
  • Password-protected PDF: remove the password first with Unlock PDF, then upload the unlocked file.
