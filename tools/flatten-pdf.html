Why flatten a PDF?

Interactive PDFs can contain form fields, comments, annotations, or digital elements that change when opened in different applications. Flattening a PDF converts those elements into standard page content, helping ensure the document looks the same everywhere.

PDFly flattens PDF files directly in your browser, keeping your documents on your device throughout — no sign-up, no watermark, no software to install.

Common uses

  • Finalize filled forms
  • Lock document appearance
  • Preserve annotations
  • Prepare files for printing
  • Prevent accidental edits

Best Practices & Limitations

Tips for best results

  • Flatten right before sharing a filled form or annotated document so the final version can't be accidentally altered.
  • Keep an unflattened copy for yourself in case you need to make edits later — flattening isn't reversible.
  • Flatten after all annotations and form fields are finalized, not before, since flattening locks in whatever's there at that point.

Limitations & when not to use this tool

  • Once flattened, form fields and annotations become plain page content and can't be edited or extracted as data again.
  • Doesn't reduce file size on its own — pair with Compress PDF if size is also a concern.
  • Not a redaction tool — flattened text is still visible and can still be copied/read, just no longer editable as a field.

Frequently asked questions

What happens when a PDF is flattened?

Interactive elements become part of the page, so they can no longer be edited separately.

Will my form data remain visible?

Yes. Filled form values stay visible after flattening, but they are no longer editable.

Can I undo a flattened PDF?

No. Flattening is permanent in the new file, so keep the original PDF if you may need future edits.

Does flattening reduce quality?

No. The document's visual appearance is preserved while making interactive content static.
